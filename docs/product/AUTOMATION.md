# From a niche to one reviewable episode

The creator chooses a niche and later watches one finished private preview. Between those decisions the factory keeps an evidence packet, a pool of scripts, an edit decision list, cover options, and a release proposal. Work runs on one machine with one render worker. Rendering a hundred videos would spend the budget at the wrong stage. Explore alternatives in text and small images, then encode the winning edit once.

## What to optimize

YouTube describes viewer choice, average viewing duration, average percentage viewed, likes and satisfaction surveys as recommendation inputs. Competition, audience interest and seasonality also affect distribution. Give the viewer a concrete question or result in the first shot. Pay it off on screen. A judge can check readability and whether the evidence supports a claim. Only real viewer behavior can validate that judgment. [YouTube Shorts discovery](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225)

A cover should communicate the same story as the opening shot. Save a cover image for verified accounts using desktop Studio and make a strong frame available in the actual Short for mobile frame selection. Track the two decisions separately. [Desktop thumbnail controls](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431) · [Mobile Shorts creation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10343433)

Audience activity can inform the first hours. A channel without timing history needs an explicit test window. A daily cadence is a maximum of one reviewed episode per day. Skip a day if the only candidate repeats yesterday's work. [YouTube performance FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805) · [Channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)

## Worker handoffs

| Worker | Input and decision | Artifact and acceptance condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research desk | Freeze the selected observation, its time, examples, counts, links and scope. Separate the actual subject from evidence of interest in it. | Research packet. Every factual beat cites a fact in this packet. A view chart ranks the sampled uploads only. |
| Script writer | For ranking/news prototypes, produce about 100 complete inexpensive edits around verified facts. Mix question, comparison, chronology, reverse reveal and concise bulletin openings. For narrative series, a later batch writer needs distinct plots and continuity across episodes. | Drafts with hook, ordered scenes, captions, payoff and estimated seconds. Count actual distinct drafts. Keep generation method visible. |
| Script editor | Reject unsupported claims, unreadable lines, unresolved promises and excessive repetition. Compare coverage, specificity and pace among eligible scripts. Retain scores and reasons. | One selected script and runners-up. Scores express editorial preference. Evaluate against observed audience metrics after release. |
| Material desk | Translate each beat into a shot request with subject, action, duration, visual role and audio cue. Search approved time-coded fragments, stills, music and effects. Check permission and relevance before aesthetic ranking. Preserve origin, license evidence, attribution and input hashes. | Edit decision list. Each used item explains what it illustrates. Fill gaps with original diagrams, motion or synthesized audio. Never infer permission from a research link or thumbnail. |
| Cover desk and editor | Generate multiple treatments using the actual episode subject, a readable promise, and original or authorized imagery. Compare mobile legibility, safe-area fit, contrast and agreement with the payoff. | Winner, alternatives and selection rationale. The first frame should also work when playback starts immediately. |
| Compiler and QA | Trim fragments to scene bounds, assemble stills and overlays, time captions and effects, mix music, and encode once. Decode the result, check dimensions, duration, caption fit and manifest completeness. | Private preview, cover, sources, attribution, costs and run report. A failed stage never enters the release queue. |
| Release planner | Predict early reach only from comparable owner-authorized observations. With insufficient history, rotate documented local-time test slots. Reserve a free day only for a reviewed artifact. | A proposed future time with timezone and rationale. A proposal has no remote side effect. |
| Publisher and feedback | At release, recheck hashes, rights, audience flag, future time and channel. Upload privately via owner OAuth, persist remote ID and reconcile uncertain outcomes before any retry. Compare performance at equal video age and by format. | Remote receipt or actionable failure. Feed actual retention and viewer-choice data into future format tests. |

## Order and operating limits

1. Build the selection loop inside the existing Produce action for rankings and briefs. Verify that the chosen script, cover and media are actually used.
2. Expand cleared material sources. Use trusted adapters with file size, protocol, license and attribution checks. Compare matching quality on a small human-reviewed set before increasing volume.
3. Add durable approval-bound upload and scheduling after live OAuth is configured. Use the documented private-video and publishAt requirements. [YouTube video resource](https://developers.google.com/youtube/data/reference/rest/v3/videos)
4. Add analytics snapshots and compare hook families and release slots at the same publication age. Keep enough exploration to avoid repeatedly selecting one lucky result.
5. Scale narrative workers after continuity, character rights, voice permissions, age suitability and per-episode originality can be checked. Avoid producing near-identical episodes at scale.

Use original music or individually cleared tracks. Record credit for each track with an attribution requirement. [YouTube Audio Library](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882)
